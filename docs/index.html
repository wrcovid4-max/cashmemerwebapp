A cash memo app that runs on your own computer, in a browser — with your phone as the barcode scanner. No app store, no monthly fee, no account required, and your takings never leave the machine unless you tell them to.

Get it on GitHub How to start it

⚠️ This page is the description, not the app

Cash Memer runs a small server on your own computer — it keeps a database, talks to your phone over a live connection, and builds PDFs. GitHub Pages can only serve fixed files, so the app itself cannot run at this address and never will.

The app runs at http://localhost:4000 on the computer you start it on. Getting there takes two commands — see below.

Starting it ↑ top

  1. Install Node.js 22.5 or newer

    From nodejs.org. Check what you have with node --version.

  2. Get the project and install it, once

    # in a terminal
git clone https://github.com/wrcovid4-max/cashmemerwebapp
cd cashmemerwebapp
npm install

  3. Start it, every time

    npm start

    Leave that window open — closing it stops the app. Press Ctrl+C to stop it on purpose.

  4. Open the address it prints

      ON THIS COMPUTER — open this in your browser:
      http://localhost:4000

  ON YOUR PHONE — same Wi-Fi as this computer:
      http://192.168.100.9:4000    <- normal use, no warning
      https://192.168.100.9:4001   <- needed for the camera

    It finds your Wi-Fi address itself. You never have to go looking for it.

Your phone is the scanner ↑ top

Press Phone scanner, point your phone camera at the QR code, and every barcode it reads lands in the receipt on your computer instantly. If the barcode matches something in your inventory it arrives priced; if it does not, you name it once and it works forever after.

It survives a real counter

Your phone locks, walks out of range, comes back. It reconnects on its own — there is no "pair again" step.

Nothing gets dropped

Scans made while the phone is offline queue on the phone. Scans made while the computer's page is reloading are held and delivered afterwards.

And nothing gets doubled

Every scan carries an id and delivery is acknowledged, so a scan can arrive late but can never be counted twice.

No camera? Still fine

The scanner page always has a type-it-by-hand box, and a code typed there reaches the computer exactly the same way.

Two pages, on purpose ↑ top

Every receipt is always two pages, and they are not the same page twice.

On page 1 — the customer's Page 2 only — yours
Customer name and email Their phone number
Items, totals, cash, change Their home address
Note 1, signature, QR Note 2 — the private one
Where the sale happened Your issuing account

Page 1 is handed across a counter and does not always stay with the person it belongs to. So it never carries a customer's phone number or home address.

The rest of it ↑ top

📊 Dashboard

Today, this month, all time. Averages, highest and lowest, top product, most-visited store, storage used.

🧾 Receipts

Search, date filters, bulk print and delete. Share, PDF, Print, Duplicate, Edit, Pin. Plus a weekly summary.

📦 Inventory

Barcode, brand, cost, price, stock and unit. Low-stock warnings. Archive rather than delete.

📋 Price list

The short row of things you ring up all day, one click onto a memo.

👥 Members

Customer directory. One pick fills name, phone, email and address on the receipt.

💱 Rates

Live exchange rates, USD base. Rates you enter by hand are never overwritten by a refresh.

🌐 English and اردو

Switch language without a reload. Urdu flips the whole layout right-to-left, not just the text.

🌓 Light and dark

Follows your system, or pick one. Built to be read at arm's length across a counter.

Your data stays yours ↑ top

Back it up on the first day, not the day you need it

Your receipts live in one file on your computer and are not in GitHub — a database of customer phone numbers does not belong in a repository. The only thing that moves your shop between machines is the JSON export.

Settings → Backup & restore → Export everything as JSON. Then turn on the daily backup and point it at a folder that syncs somewhere else. It keeps the newest 30 snapshots and prunes the rest by itself.

Every API key is optional. With none of them the app starts normally and each screen says exactly which key is missing and where to get a free one. Nothing phones home.
